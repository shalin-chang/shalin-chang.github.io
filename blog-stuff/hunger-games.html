The Hunger Games Trilogy: My Thoughts

I somehow never read these books until after my first year in college, despite them being favorites for many of my closest friends throughout grade school. I watched all the movies with Scotty, starting over winter break in January 2026 and finishing Mockingjay Part 2 sometime in May 2026. I'd previously seen only the first fifteen minutes of Mockingjay Part 2 while at my grandparents' house. I had a somewhat underwhelmed reaction to the movies, having come in with certain expectations and missing some context, so I'll start with my initial impressions of the films and then compare them to their respective books.

Thoughts

For each movie, I'll include my half-joking, half-serious Letterboxd reviews as I always write them immediately after watching with no significant reflection. As such, I believe them to be the most accurate depictions of my initial thoughts on the movies, so after each one, I'll elaborate on those initial thoughts and then go into further reflection. One note before continuing; every single review, regardless of my opinions on the film, will include the phrase "fantastic movie." This is a joke and should not be interpreted beyond this.

The Hunger Games (2012)

Letterboxd Review, 1/1/2026 (3.5 stars): I've never read the books before and the only movie I've seen is part of The Mockingjay Part 2, so I was going into this almost completely blind. Other than the momentously stupid decision to name that guy "Peeta" (c'mon now) I was pleasantly surprised at the quality of this fantastic movie while watching with my favorite chud. I suppose the quantity of books and movies about cartoonish capitalist dystopias (no, the word government does not make this a critique of communism) and plain-faced, overpowered protagonists blundering through their love angles put me off the concept, but since this is one of the OGs I guess it did the concept a little better before it was diluted so much. This experience transported me to a long-lost era where this movie was fresh, the concept not overdone and the people's hearts could feel joy and love. Well, some of us still can. I love you.

So obviously, this movie holds some sentimentality for me due to the circumstances under which I watched it. On the other hand, you can also see some snark due to a few of the blatant parallels between THG and Twilight, with their pairs of stock love interests in which one man represents warmth and the other one is problematically old.

Design. Part of THG's premise is that the tributes are fed, prettied up, and then shown off on national television to garner attention and attraction from viewers, who will then get to root for their personal favorites.

I can't remember the movie very well but the book does so much more work with characterization. From the movie, Katniss is clearly shown as strong, focused on survival, and new to the concept of love. I think it does a good job focusing on Katniss' relationship with prim even when she's not in the scene. The movie can only allude to this when she's there or when Katniss yells "Prim!!" But in the books she's in her internal monologue constantly Glimmer- can't be older than 18 but goes for the sexy look to appeal to the Capitol viewers. Her body gets mutilated by the wasps and further in Katniss' mind from the hallucinogens. Again, a very unserious and snide review made in the haze of confusion which plagued my viewing. I legitimately couldn't remember how many movies there were, and the main plot of "whoops, you're in the Hunger Games again, but now it's somehow worse" felt like a cheap way to rehash what had already happened. I think I just haven't watched a movie series in a while because almost all sequels do some variation of the first movie's plot but bigger/badder/worse. Perhaps I was hoping for a Star Wars-esque trilogy in which the second movie is transitionary moment, for both the larger conflict and the protagonist's development.

Recurring sensory motifs in both books that are hard or impossible to show onscreen. In the first book, Katniss spends a good few chapters deaf in one ear following an explosion that ruptured her eardrum. In catching fire, she smells blood on Snow's breath, and while he does cough it up during the mockingjay movies and it's eventually explained, this sensory thing is much clearer and also subtler. I think part of it, especially the deafness, is intended to highlight that her senses and own actions are all she has that she can control, and even then they're subject to interference or manipulation. Weakness of human condition Im halfway through catching fire and I think I'm understanding that the Hunger games is on its surface a political allegory and its deeper message is more about Katniss' personal struggle.
