Breaking Bad Notes

6.25.2026 - I started watching this show for the first time this month. Outside of various pop culture references, clips online, and quotes by my high school chemistry teacher who wanted to be as badass as Walt really bad, I didn't know much about the show. I assumed it would be a straightforward male power fantasy about making money and shooting people but I noticed a bunch of cool things going on. Since it's rare that I get to visit a piece of media with such little information going in, I thought I'd continue staying away from any existing discourse and make my own thoughts, based only on the aforementioned pre-knowledge and my thoughts while watching. If my points have already been talked about to death in an hour-long YouTube video, I take that as a sign that I noticed important themes and ideas and wasn't just making things up. If you disagree with anything I say, feel free to email me your complaints.

Season 1

Skylar x Walter

- One of the few things I knew about the show before watching was that Skylar is supposed to commonly interpreted as a nagging bitch who keeps interfering with Walter's business ventures. I also know that Walt gets a lot bigger in his dealings than the comparatively small operations that he and Jesse work on in S1, thanks to YouTube Shorts and also the fact that this goes on for five more seasons. I feel like Skylar's negative interpretation is the result of male viewers idolizing Walt as some kind of badass instead of what the writers seem to be going for, which is setting him up to be a chud who makes profoundly terrible decisions out of pride and a desire to break from his perceived failed life. Skylar is being as perfect a wife one could ask for, barely complaining about her pregnancy and instead spending all her time trying to help Walt sort himself out. The near-total lack of focus on the hardships of pregnancy is somewhat interesting as either a male writer's oversight or a deliberate focus on Walt and marginalizing all other struggles?

- Sex- Walt gets horny more and more often as the season progresses, to the point of touching Skylar under a table in a crowded school board meeting. Skylar seems to appreciate this as a welcome change over his apathy to his birthday handjob at the very beginning.

Marie and the Importance of Gossip

- I'm pretty sure the entire Skylar-Marie-Hank relationship is set up to show how quickly information passes from one member of this extended family to another, raising the stakes and possibilities for Walt to get found out. A casual remark by Skylar to Marie about the effects of someone smoking pot results in Marie telling Hank, who then drags Walt Jr (whose name I keep forgetting is Walt Jr- he looks like a Nick or a Luis to me) to chit-chat with a meth'd up prostitute. Many scenes include repeated information as each household conveys information back and forth; we see, for example, Skylar talk about returning the tiara to Walt, then returning it, then confronting Marie about having stolen it, then telling Walt that Marie wouldn't admit to it. We see the same information being told by one person to another, sometimes three or four times, as it slowly spreads through the various social circles. Walt's cancer diagnosis is one of the most central examples. In Season 1, he is first told by a doctor, and then tells Skylar (off-screen), Crazy-8, and Marie, Hank and Walt Jr (due to Skylar having brought it up). Eliot and his wife admit to knowing about the cancer from Skylar having told them (off-screen), and Jesse figures it out on his own based on recognizing the redness on Walt's chest as being a radiation target. The show's incessant telling and retelling of information and the reactions, based on the stakes of each situation, I think are meant to keep maintaining the tension that Walt has to be careful so as to avoid anyone finding out about his actions; telling one wrong person will eventually get that information back to Hank. Every time the extended family convenes, it's meant to raise the audience's anxieties that their combined information could be enough to figure out what Walt was up to; Hank knows that Walt suddenly got interested in visiting a meth lab and also got better at lying (see the poker game), and Skylar could have compared notes and seen that Walt started hanging out with Jesse a lot right after that.
