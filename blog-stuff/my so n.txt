<p>He looks so peaceful.</p>
<p>First a psychiatrist, then a respected lawyer, then the organizer of a massive city-wide charity, he now shuffles in hesitant, awkward steps towards his usual spot.</p>
<p>His existence is crumbling, and yet he looks so peaceful.</p>
<p>He sits at the head of the white marble table, a ceremonial setting- a vestige from a time where he would talk animatedly with his family over a home-cooked dinner. Now he sits quietly, and he looks so peaceful. He no longer needs his glasses to aid his soft, dark eyes, leaving them unobstructed and unfocused, his gaze lost in the steam rising from several take-out containers filled with char siu bao and beef skewers. One of his large front teeth has pushed its way forward in his gums, such that it now protrudes past his lips like a caricature of an infant with its fresh new incisor.</p>
<p>Talk to him, and his buck tooth will grow with his smile as he peers up into your face. Thank him for his kindness, his love, his teachings, and he will instinctively say, "you're welcome." Maybe he even knows that your face is supposed to mean something special to him. His sons are all there, all three of them. Despite being three decades their senior, he still has more hair than any of them. Their sparse widows' peaks shine in the light cast by the dining room chandelier. They sit at the table with all their children, eager to break into the meal. His hairline is unchanged from how it was when his first grandson was born nineteen years ago. He can't remember to comb it, yet decades of practice keep his neat combover intact.</p>
<br>
<p>So many smiling, young faces. Even their parents look young. They gather eagerly around the steaming takeout containers, pulling apart the dumplings that had gotten stuck to each other in the car ride home as they chattered to each other. They pile food so high onto their plates, it threatens to spill. They say they got their appetites from me. I laugh, uncertain. I don't eat much today. I don't remember yesterday, but I can't imagine I ate much more then either. Perhaps, in some long-ago time, when I could bathe by myself and connect each of these faces to a name. Now it's a fun challenge- one of the women begins pointing across the table at each person in turn, quizzing me on their identities. I gamely play along, squinting at the teenage girl squirming awkwardly in the chair directly across from me.</p>
<p>"Yeh-Yeh, do you know who this is?"</p>
<p>I don't. I smile in apology. She doesn't seem to take it personally, toying with her mango cake </p>
